    Why not wait for Black Friday the earlier plan

    The first guide said to buy between November 23 and December 1. That was based on Google's data (prices usually lowest 54 to 78 days before departure) and the timing of Canadian airline sales. Two things changed it:

    • Sales that cover February are running now. Last Black Friday, the Family Day weekend was blacked out but normal February weeks qualified, so waiting gains little.
    • About two-thirds of these cheap fares is taxes and fees, and sales only discount the base fare. A 20 to 25% sale saves roughly $100 to $150 for the four of us. Losing the 7 remaining seats at the lowest WestJet fare would cost more.

    If we haven't bought by Nov 24 to Dec 1 (Black Friday to Travel Tuesday), buy that week regardless. Porter's Boxing Week sale (Dec 26 to 31, up to 25% off base fares last year) only matters if we're on Porter.

    Time of day and booking day of the week have no reliable effect. What matters is the departure day: Tuesdays beat Saturdays by $200 to $2,300.

    Destination profiles beaches, hospitals, groceries, where to base

    Puerto Vallarta area

    • Nonstops: Air Canada, WestJet, Air Transat, Porter (Oct 23 to Apr 30) and Flair. About 5h45 out, under 5h back. Porter also flies from Hamilton and sometimes Toronto Island.
    • Base: north Bucerías to Tizate has the calmest small-wave beach on the bay and a cluster of new small buildings. Nuevo Vallarta is flatter and gentler but dominated by big timeshare resorts. Marina Vallarta is 5 to 10 minutes from the airport, cheaper, weaker beach. Zona Romántica is great for dinner, hilly for a stroller.
    • Transfer: 15 to 25 minutes.
    • Medical and groceries: excellent. Private hospitals; Mega, La Comer, Walmart, Costco, Soriana.
    • Watch: the February 22, 2026 cartel violence in Jalisco brought a shelter-in-place order and two days of cancelled flights. Book refundable, insure for trip interruption. The current advisory doesn't cover the resort zone.

    Huatulco

    • Nine bays, most sheltered. Santa Cruz, La Entrega, Chahué, the east end of Tangolunda and Arrocito are calm and shallow. February: 33°C highs, 19°C lows, water about 27°C, almost no rain.
    • Air Canada about twice weekly (Tue, Sat), WestJet weekly (Fri), departures about 9:30 a.m. Few flights mean rigid dates and fares that climb fast. Medical care is basic for anything serious.
    • Stays: condos in Chahué, Santa Cruz or Tangolunda at $180 to $350 a night; Barceló Huatulco (water park, kids' club 4 to 12, from $366 a night, Ocean Front room sleeps 4 max). See backups for the gems.

    Cozumel west coast

    • The hotel-zone side, where El Cozumeleño is, is sheltered and usually much cleaner than the east. February is normally fine. The risk is another early-season event like January 2026. If the December USF bulletin shows the western Caribbean filling up early, skip it.
    • About 4h, 10 to 15 minutes to the hotel zone. Small island; bigger hospitals are in Cancún.

    Aruba

    • "Take normal security precautions." Eagle, Palm, Druif and Manchebo beaches stay clean; December to February is the annual low for seaweed. Baby Beach is a big, shallow lagoon behind a breakwater.
    • February is peak season: 2-bedroom condos $350 to $600 a night, dinners $150 to $250, groceries 1.5 times Canadian prices. Online ED card needed before flying. 1 hour ahead of Toronto.

    Los Cabos

    • Dry and sunny, 24 to 26°C, water about 22°C (chilly for a baby). Only Médano, Chileno, Santa María and Tequila Cove are reliably safe; the Pacific side is dangerous. Whale season.
    • San José del Cabo is 15 to 20 minutes from the airport; rentals $300 to $550 a night. Food and rentals run 20 to 30% above Vallarta. 2 hours behind Toronto.

    Also looked at

    • Guanacaste, Costa Rica: dry, hot, seaweed-free; Playa Hermosa and Flamingo are gentle. But "high degree of caution due to crime," one of the world's highest road accident rates, and 25 to 75 minute transfers.
    • Florida: 3-hour flight, Porter daily from Hamilton, level 1. Water 22 to 23°C, pool rentals pricey in February, US medical bills need serious insurance.
    • Cancún / Isla Mujeres: frequent nonstops (Porter from Hamilton 6 times a week). Seaweed is beach by beach; Playa Norte, Costa Mujeres and the north Hotel Zone are the protected spots.
    • Playa del Carmen / Riviera Maya: lots of flights and stays, worst seaweed exposure on the coast.
    • Punta Cana / Puerto Plata: many family all-inclusives; Punta Cana's east coast is exposed. Bayahibe and La Romana usually do better.

    Dropped

    • Ixtapa-Zihuatanejo: Guerrero is "avoid non-essential travel," with an exception only when arriving by air. Too fragile with a baby, and no current nonstop from Toronto.
    • Mazatlán: Sinaloa has regular armed clashes; only listed tourist zones are excepted.
    • Manzanillo: no nonstop, and Colima is under an advisory outside the city.
    Sargassum: what it is and how to check the deciding factor for the Caribbean

    Sargassum is a floating brown seaweed. When big mats wash onto beaches they turn the water brown, make getting in unpleasant and smell like eggs as they rot. It isn't uniform across an island: wind, currents, which way the shore faces and daily cleanup all change it.

    2026 was a near-record year. USF called it likely "another major sargassum year." In January 2026, strong winds pushed an out-of-season landing onto beaches from Cancún to Xcalak; Playa del Carmen crews removed 22.5 tonnes in one afternoon. USF's June bulletin reported record-high amounts for June (about 33.6 million tonnes across all regions). By September, amounts were declining but still above the 75th percentile everywhere except the east Atlantic.

    February is the low season, not a guarantee. No forecast for February 2027 exists yet; USF only issues month-ahead outlooks. Pacific destinations remove the risk.

    Before we go

    Bottom line

    Health

    • Measles. Cases were reported in Bahía de Banderas, where Bucerías is. The baby's 12-month MMR needs to be at least 2 weeks before Feb 9. Ask whether the 4-year-old can get the second dose early (Toronto Public Health allows it before travel). Check Grandma's immunity. Ask about hepatitis A.
    • Pharmacy bag: children's acetaminophen and ibuprofen with syringes, rehydration salts, thermometer, mineral sunscreen (expensive in Mexico), icaridin repellent (preferred for babies over 6 months; DEET limited to 10%, once a day). Bring any medication: counterfeit medicine is a known problem.
    • Swim shirts, mosquito net for the pack-and-play, bottled water for bottles.

    Documents and money

    • Every traveller, including the baby, needs a passport valid for the whole stay. Apply now; baby photos take patience.
    • If either parent, or Grandma alone, flies with the kids on any leg: the Government of Canada consent letter (notarized if possible) plus long-form birth certificates.
    • Mexico uses a digital FMM on arrival; keep the confirmation.
    • No-foreign-fee card: most Canadian cards add 2.5%. Scotiabank Passport, Scotiabank Gold Amex and Home Trust Preferred Visa don't.
    • Phone: several Canadian plans include Mexico from about $35 to $40 a month; daily roaming is about $18 a day. Switch one phone.

    Insurance

    • OHIP covers almost nothing abroad. Travel medical with cancellation and interruption for everyone, given the February 2026 flight disruption.
    • Credit card coverage often has age caps and trip-length limits. Grandma likely needs her own policy with a pre-existing condition stability clause.
    • Many policies exclude regions under "avoid non-essential travel." Paying with a gift card can void card insurance.

    Getting around

    • Airport van: US$140 to $160 each way. Car seats are "on request" at most companies, so get it in writing or bring our own (gate-checked free). Uber can't pick up inside PVR and won't have seats.
    • Baby gear rental: Baby Vallarta and Baby Bliss deliver to Bucerías. Crib, high chair and jogging stroller about $350 for the week.

    13 · Reference

    Fine print and glossary

    Bottom line

    No HST on flights to Mexico either way. Price-drop guarantees are mostly gone, which is one more reason to book flights and condo separately.

    HST, TICO, fees and guarantees
    • HST: flights to Mexico are zero-rated. In a package, the Mexican hotel and tours aren't taxed either. Only Canadian pieces (some agency fees, domestic legs) carry HST. Route choice saves almost nothing on tax.
    • TICO protection only applies when booking through an Ontario-registered agency or tour operator, and it repays what you paid, not replacement travel. Booking direct with the airline gets the 24-hour refund but no TICO cover.
    • Mexico's non-resident fee (in the airfare) is 983 pesos now; the 2027 budget proposes 1,334.80, with votes due by Oct 31. Buying in 2026 might lock the old rate. About $25 a seat and unconfirmed, so don't let it drive timing.
    • Price-drop guarantees: Sunwing ended its guarantee in 2024; WestJet Vacations has none; Air Canada Vacations has one only with its $99-a-person CareFree add-on, capped at $200 a person, claimed by phone.
    • Packages and kids: Air Transat's Kids Stay Free means "one child stays free for each paying adult," but airfare and taxes still apply and age limits vary by hotel. Resort child-rate age bands vary (WestJet Vacations uses 5 to 11 for Barceló Huatulco). Check whether the baby counts toward room occupancy.
    Listing words that hide things
    All-inclusive
    Meals and some drinks and activities bundled. Confirm restaurants, kids' meals, reservations, child charges and whether flights are actually included.
    Room occupancy
    The maximum guests allowed, not the number of beds. A "family room" doesn't guarantee a separate bedroom.
    Junior suite vs 1-bedroom
    A junior suite is often one big open room. A 1-bedroom suite should have a wall and a door. Check the floor plan.
    Nonstop vs direct
    Nonstop has no landings. "Direct" can include a stop. Confirm 0 stops.
    Infant in arms
    Under 2 can fly without a seat, fees may apply. Transport Canada strongly recommends a child restraint.
    Beachfront vs beach access
    Beachfront is on the shore but may not be safe to swim. Beach access might mean a long walk, a road crossing or a shuttle.
    Cancellation policy
    The last free-cancellation date and the penalties after it. It matters if seaweed, family health or airfare changes.
    Total price
    All required lodging fees plus flights, transfers, meals, bags, child and infant charges, taxes and expected tips.
