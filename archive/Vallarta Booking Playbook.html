Does the day of week or time of day matter?

No reliable effect. Expedia's 2026 Canadian data names Monday as the cheapest booking day, but the US and UK editions name different days with 2 to 3% gaps, and Google says the booking day "matters little." What matters is the departure day: Tuesdays here beat Saturdays by $200 to $2,300. The only clock times that matter are sale deadlines: Porter and Air Canada end at 11:59 pm Eastern, WestJet at 11:59 pm Mountain.

70,000 Aeroplan points

Use the points for Grandma or one leg, not the whole family

Against WestJet's cash fare, a point is worth about 1¢ here. Save them for Grandma, where they're worth 1.5¢ or more.

Aeroplan prices Air Canada flights by demand, so the points price follows the cash fare. Air Canada's own site shows YYZ→PVR one-way from 12,500 points + about $230 in taxes, and up to 29,000 at busy times. Three seats both ways would take about 75,000 to 120,000 points, so 70K won't cover the whole family.

Recommendation. Pay cash for the four of you on WestJet. Save the points for Grandma. A last-minute cash seat gets expensive, while Aeroplan only charges more when the cash fare rises too. If she decides late, a one-way at 12,500 to 20,000 points beats a $500-plus cash fare.

When points do win for the family: when the Air Canada one-way prices near 12,500 points and one of you has an Aeroplan co-branded credit card (TD, CIBC or Amex). That card gives a free first checked bag to up to 8 people on the same booking, which wipes out WestJet's bag fees. A lap infant on an Aeroplan booking costs only about $25 or 2,500 points each way, versus about $400 on an Air Canada cash ticket.

How to check, and the rule of thumb
  • Value per point is the cash you'd otherwise pay, minus the award taxes, divided by the points. Published valuations put an Aeroplan point at about 1.4 to 1.6¢, so anything under about 1.2¢ is a poor use.
  • How to check: on aircanada.com (Canadian site), tick "Book with Aeroplan points", search one-way Feb 9 and one-way Feb 16 separately, and log in as the cardholder to see member pricing.
  • Rule of thumb: redeem only if the points per one-way seat are at most (cash one-way − taxes) ÷ 0.012. Against WestJet's ~$220 one-way, that's under about 9,000 points, which Air Canada rarely offers. Against Air Canada's ~$325 one-way, it's under about 17,500.
  • Safety net: award bookings can be cancelled free within 24 hours. After that, expect about $100 per direction to change and about $125 to $150 per ticket to cancel.
  • Don't buy points to top up. Even on sale they cost about 1.8¢ each, more than they're worth on this route.

Cheap breakfasts, street food, nicer dinners

Food plan, built around L.

Corn tortillas, ceviche and grilled fish are naturally safe. The traps are hidden: Maggi and soy sauce (wheat) in marinades, chicken stock and lard in rice and beans.

L. is pescatarian and gluten-free. This plan treats her gluten-free needs as strict (coeliac-level) because that's the safe default. If a few crumbs don't bother her, the street-food list gets longer.

Usually safe for L.

  • Ceviche and green aguachile on corn tostadas. Ask for no Maggi.
  • Whole grilled fish al mojo de ajo (garlic) or plain a la talla.
  • Grilled fish or shrimp tacos on 100% corn tortillas.
  • Shrimp cocktail. Ask about Worcestershire sauce.
  • Guacamole, pico de gallo, fresh salsas, fruit with chile and lime.
  • Whole beans de la olla and plain white rice, once she's confirmed no lard or chicken stock.

Avoid or ask first

  • Battered fish ("estilo Ensenada"), anything empanizado (breaded), and fried chips or tostadas from a shared fryer.
  • Black aguachile (negro) and micheladas: soy, Maggi and Worcestershire.
  • Pescado zarandeado, the local grilled-fish dish: many marinades use soy or Maggi. Ask for it without.
  • Red Mexican rice and fideo soup: chicken stock, and the soup has wheat noodles.
  • Refried beans and tamales: usually pork lard.
  • Mole, flour tortillas, beer.

L.'s dining card

Groceries: breakfast and lunch at the condo

  • Main shop: Mega, on the highway at the south edge of Bucerías, open 7 am to 11 pm. La Comer, across the road, has more imported and gluten-free products. Walmart, near Flamingos, is cheapest. Uber Eats delivers groceries in Bucerías if you'd rather not go out on arrival day.
  • Fresh fish: Mercado del Mar at La Cruz marina is open daily. About 30 stalls fillet snapper, mahi-mahi and tuna to order. Go before 9 am. Two condo fish dinners cost a fraction of a restaurant.
  • Markets: the La Cruz Sunday market (Sunday Feb 14, about 9 am to 2 pm) and the Bucerías Sunday street market for fruit, shrimp and snacks.
  • First shop for the week, about $400–550: milk, eggs, yogurt, two boxes of cereal, oats for the rest of you, bananas, papaya, pineapple, avocados, corn tortillas from a tortilla shop, cheese, peanut butter, juice, coffee, drinking water in big 20-litre jugs, diapers, baby pouches, and produce-washing drops (Microdyn).

Pack from home for L., because certified gluten-free oats weren't found in Mexican stores and "sin gluten" labels there are often unreliable: certified GF oats (1 to 1.5 kg), her usual GF cereal, GF crackers or bread, one box of GF pasta, a bottle of GF tamari to hand to a chef, and snack bars for the beach.

Mexican customs allow sealed, shop-labelled, shelf-stable food. Don't bring fresh fruit, sandwiches or cheese. Bring a full week of the baby's formula too, because Mexican versions are made differently.

Backup destinations

Huatulco has the best toddler beaches but costly, infrequent flights. In Los Cabos, only Médano Beach is calm enough.

Huatulco

Santa Cruz bay is the calm, toddler-friendly beach. EOS Suites (about $244/night, 2BR, fenced pool with a shallow end and baby gates, but only 22 reviews) and Nabani Condominios (about $375, 9.4 from 133 reviews, a 9-minute walk to the beach) are the gems. Camino Real Zaashila's condos (about $280) put a kitchen on a private, sheltered beach. Flights are the problem: Feb 6–13 was about $4,500 for four. Lock flights first there.

Los Cabos

The San José del Cabo hotel-zone beach is not swimmable. Lumina at Marea (about $304, 2BR, 9.5, a 6-minute walk to Médano; ask for a high unit away from the noisy street) and Condo Paraiso (about $326, 9.6, quieter, 12-minute walk) are the picks. Avoid Villa del Palmar and Pueblo Bonito for timeshare pressure. Whale-watching catamarans run about US$75 to $130 per person.

HST, fees and protection

The fine print that moves the total

There's almost no HST on a Mexico trip either way. Book flights and condo separately; package price-drop guarantees are mostly gone.

  • HST: flights to Mexico are zero-rated, so no 13% HST. In a package, the Mexican hotel and tours aren't taxed either. Only Canadian pieces (some agency service fees, domestic legs) can carry HST.
  • TICO protection only applies if you book through an Ontario-registered agency or tour operator, and it repays what you paid, not replacement travel. Booking flights direct with the airline gets the 24-hour refund but no TICO coverage.
  • Mexico's non-resident fee (in the airfare) is 983 pesos now. The 2027 budget proposes 1,334.80, with votes due by Oct 31. Buying in 2026 might lock the old rate. That's about $25 per paid seat and unconfirmed, so don't let it drive the timing.
  • Price-drop guarantees are mostly gone: Sunwing ended its guarantee in 2024 and WestJet Vacations has none. Air Canada Vacations has one only with its $99-per-person CareFree add-on, capped at $200 per person, and you must claim it by phone.
  • Porter tip: Costco sells $500 Porter e-gift cards for $449.99, and they stack with Porter sales. Paying with a gift card can void your credit card's travel insurance, so buy insurance separately.
