    Decide

    At a glance

    Bottom line

    Go Pacific. A 2-bedroom condo in Nuevo Vallarta or Bucerías is the best value with no seaweed risk. Hold the condo now, buy flights November 23 to December 1, and fly Tuesday to Tuesday.

    Top pickPuerto Vallarta areaNuevo Vallarta or Bucerías. Ranked first in both reports.

    The short version

    • Pacific coast removes the seaweed question. Puerto Vallarta, Huatulco and Los Cabos are outside the sargassum system. Caribbean beaches are usually fine in February, but 2026 was a near-record year with an out-of-season January landing.
    • First choice: Nuevo Vallarta or Bucerías. Five nonstop airlines from Pearson, a 15 to 25 minute transfer, big grocery stores, good private hospitals and lots of 2-bedroom condos.
    • Second: Huatulco for the calmest bays, if its roughly three flights a week line up. Third depends on budget: Aruba if you can spend 35 to 45% more for the lowest risk, or Cozumel's west coast if you want familiar and cheaper.
    • Sleep behind a real door. A 2-bedroom condo lets the kids nap and go down early while you still have a living room. Adding 2 resort nights costs about $400 to $900 more than a pure rental.
    • Cook breakfast and lunch, spend on dinner. About $1,000 to $1,700 a week in Puerto Vallarta, which usually beats the all-inclusive markup of $1,050 to $2,100 a week.
    • Fly Tuesday to Tuesday. Live fares for February 2 to 9, 9 to 16 and 16 to 23 are about $1,630 to $1,760 for the four of you. Leaving Saturday, February 13 costs about $4,010.
    • Do three things now: apply for the baby's passport, hold a 2-bedroom condo with free cancellation, and price travel medical insurance that covers Grandma by name.

    Trip brief

    Bottom line

    One nonstop flight, a calm swimmable beach and pool, a door between the kids and the adults, and a kitchen. Compare options door to door, not by nightly rate.

    What "affordable" should mean

    • Compare the door-to-door total, not the hotel's nightly price or the airfare per person.
    • Leave a buffer of about $250 to $500 for excursions, transport changes or baby costs.
    • Don't pay for two resort rooms if a well-located 2-bedroom condo gives you the same privacy.

    Decide

    Sargassum

    Bottom line

    February is the low season, not a guarantee. The Pacific coast removes the risk; Cozumel's west side and Aruba lower it.

    Sargassum is a floating brown seaweed. In open water it supports marine life. When large mats wash onto tourist beaches they spoil swimming and can smell as they rot.

    How it affects a beach day

    • Looks: piles or floating mats turn clear blue water brown.
    • Swimming: heavy build-up makes getting in unpleasant and hides shallow water.
    • Smell: rotting seaweed gives off gases with an egg-like odour. Avoid heavy decomposition, especially with young children.
    • Variability: it is not uniform across an island. Wind, currents, which way the shore faces and daily cleanup all change conditions.

    What 2026 looked like

    • The University of South Florida (USF) Optical Oceanography Lab called 2026 likely "another major sargassum year," and reported exceptionally high regional amounts in February 2026, with possible beaching along the Mexican Caribbean coast.
    • In January 2026, strong winds pushed an "out of season" landing onto beaches from Cancún to Xcalak. Playa del Carmen crews removed 22.5 tonnes in one afternoon; Isla Mujeres crews removed about 220 tonnes from Playa Norte "in a few hours."
    • USF's June 2026 bulletin: "the Caribbean Sea continued to show record-high sargassum amount for the month of June," about 33.6 million metric tons across all monitored regions.
    • USF's September 2026 bulletin: amounts declining "in almost every region," but still above the 75th percentile of historical levels everywhere except the east Atlantic. The western Caribbean held steady. USF expects declines "until possibly November."

    How to check before a nonrefundable booking

    • Read the USF Sargassum Watch bulletins for December 2026 and January 2027 (released at the end of each month). They are the best early read for the Caribbean, but they cover regions, not beaches.
    • Look at NOAA's daily inundation risk maps closer to departure. Satellite maps show regional risk, not your particular beach.
    • Find recent, date-stamped beach photos or resort webcam footage for the exact property.
    • Ask the property: "How has seaweed affected your beach in February in recent years? Do you clear it daily?"
    Decision rule

    If avoiding seaweed matters more than a perfectly turquoise Caribbean beach, put Puerto Vallarta and Huatulco ahead of the Riviera Maya.

    What happened in Puerto Vallarta in February 2026

    On February 22, 2026, the leader of the Jalisco New Generation Cartel was killed. Burning roadblocks followed, Puerto Vallarta was put under a shelter-in-place order, and Air Canada and WestJet cancelled flights. Both airlines resumed full service from Toronto on Tuesday, February 24, after consulting the Government of Canada and Mexican authorities.

    What it means for you: Puerto Vallarta is still a reasonable choice, but book flexible or refundable options and buy insurance with trip interruption. The flare-up showed how quickly flights can stop.

    Before you go

    • Check travel.gc.ca before booking and again before departure.
    • Register with Registration of Canadians Abroad.
    • Many insurance policies do not cover regions under "avoid non-essential travel." Another reason to skip Ixtapa and Mazatlán.

    All-inclusive packages vs booking it yourself

    • A February 2027 package at a family 4-star in Puerto Vallarta or Huatulco: roughly $7,000 to $10,000 for 4 people, 7 nights, standard room. A family suite or two rooms with Grandma: about $9,500 to $14,000. Live February 2027 prices were not posted yet.
    • Air Transat Kids Stay Free: "One child stays free for each paying adult," but "airfare and taxes apply for kids," and age limits (under 7, 12 or 13) vary by hotel.
    • A "free" child still pays flight and taxes. In one Sunwing Barceló Huatulco example (2019, from Calgary), each "free" child really cost about $595. Lap infants usually pay only a small fee or taxes.
    • Child-rate age bands vary (WestJet Vacations uses 5 to 11 for Barceló Huatulco). Check whether the baby counts toward the room's maximum occupancy.
    Budget rule

    Don't compare a $2,200 condo with a $4,000 all-inclusive until you add 3 ticketed round trips, infant fees, food, transfers, taxes, bags and insurance. Resort rates in the lodging tables do not include flights.

    7 nights or 10?

    Why 7 nights may win

    • More flexibility, especially with seasonal nonstops.
    • Less spent on groceries and fewer days of car or taxi costs.
    • More room for an upgraded 2-bedroom unit or Grandma's flight.

    When 10 nights wins

    • When airfare is almost the same, three more nights in a cheap condo is a good upgrade.
    • When the return flight is nonstop on day 10 and the apartment has room to make downtime enjoyable.

    Where to stay

    Bottom line

    A 2-bedroom condo with a real door is the sweet spot. Add 1 or 2 resort nights at the start or end if you want the vacation feel.

    All-inclusive

    • Lodging, buffet and meals, drinks and on-site activities in one price. Flights only if sold as a package.
    • Kids' clubs, transfers and no planning.
    • A standard room may leave no adult space after bedtime.

    Condo or Airbnb

    • Kitchen, separate bedrooms, a living room and laundry. Better for five.
    • You pay for groceries, restaurants, transfers and maybe cleaning fees (add 12 to 18% for platform and cleaning fees).

    Suite resort or hybrid

    • Apartment-style floor plan with hotel pools and services. Often the best comfort compromise if the 1-bedroom rate is reasonable.
    • Or split the stay: resort nights plus a condo. See Hybrid plans.

    Sleeping layouts

    The layout matters more than the square footage. Ask for photos or a floor plan that shows a real door between the kids' room and the adult space.

    LayoutWho sleeps where, and the trade-off
    One-bedroom suiteKids in the bedroom (crib plus a separate bed if allowed); adults in the living room after bedtime, sleeping on the sofa bed. Only works with a decent mattress and fire-safe occupancy.
    Two-bedroom condoKids in one room, parents in the other, lounge stays free. Better privacy and naps with a 4-year-old and a baby.
    Two-bedroom + GrandmaConfirm an adult-sized bed for Grandma, proper beds for the parents and a real crib. Don't rely on a flimsy sofa bed or count the crib as an adult bed.
    Standard resort roomUsually the worst fit: everyone sleeps in the same space at 8:30 p.m. A balcony next door doesn't count as a safe adult hangout while kids sleep.

    Grandma changes the math

    • Three adults means 4 ticketed seats including the 4-year-old, plus the lap infant. Airfare rises by about one full fare.
    • A 2- or 3-bedroom unit may keep lodging almost the same if its official capacity includes five guests. Check the listing's child and infant occupancy rules.
    • Grandma can offer some childcare and adult time, but don't build the trip around babysitting unless she wants that role.
    After-bedtime test

    "At 8:30 p.m., can two adults talk, read, snack or watch a movie with the lights on while both kids sleep behind a closed door?" If not, keep looking.

    Family features to ask about

    Sleeping and comfort

    • Closed bedroom door
    • Crib or pack-and-play (confirm it's a real one)
    • Beds of usable width
    • Quiet AC in the sleeping area
    • Blackout curtains
    • Living room light and reliable Wi-Fi

    Safety and logistics

    • Pool barriers and shallow areas (fencing is rare in Mexico)
    • Stairs and balcony railings; ground or low floor
    • Elevator or stroller access
    • Drinking-water plan, high chair, laundry
    • Transfer with child seats, nearby pharmacy

    Message to send a host or property

    The food math

    • Self-catering plus dinners out: about $1,000 to $1,700 a week for your family in Puerto Vallarta.

    Lap infant rules

    Air Canada

    • Lap infant pays 10% of the adult fare; taxes, fees and surcharges are charged in full (about $60 to $150).
    • Bassinets only on widebody planes (787, 777, A330), can't be confirmed until departure, and only for babies under 12 kg who can't sit up. Yours won't qualify, and most sun flights are 737s.
    • Stroller and car seat checked free.

    WestJet

    • "On most routes, lap-held infants don't pay a base fare or taxes" (about $0 to $50 in destination taxes).
    • Two infant items (stroller, car seat or playpen) checked free on top of your bags.
    • Only one lap infant per row because of oxygen masks, so plan seats so each adult with a child sits together.
    • Free adjacent seats for kids under 12 if you contact them after booking.

    The 4-year-old needs a full seat. Transport Canada strongly recommends an approved child restraint even for children under 2; if fares are low, a paid seat for the baby with a car seat is worth it.

    Flying with a toddler and a lap baby

    • Fly Porter or Flair only if you're fine paying for bags and seat selection. Porter's seats and service are more family-friendly.
    • Pick a morning departure. Both Huatulco flights leave Toronto around 9:25 to 9:30 a.m. and land early afternoon, which works with naps.
    • Gate-check the stroller and car seat. Bring a cheap umbrella stroller; Vallarta's cobblestones are hard on good ones.
    • Five to six hours with a 13 to 14 month old on your lap is the hard part. Pack new small toys, snacks, a tablet with downloaded shows, and a bottle or sippy cup for takeoff and landing.
    • "Direct" can include a stop. Confirm 0 stops.

    Other Toronto-area airports

    • Hamilton (YHM), Porter, winter 2026-27: Cancún 6 times a week, Fort Lauderdale daily, Orlando daily, Montego Bay twice a week from December 20, 2026. The airport also mentioned Puerto Vallarta returning. Parking is easier and cheaper than at Pearson.

    Documents and insurance

    Bottom line

    Everyone needs a passport, including the baby. OHIP covers almost nothing abroad, so insure all five, with trip interruption.

    Documents

    • Every traveller, including the baby, needs a Canadian passport. Mexico requires it to be valid for the length of your stay.
    • If one parent, or Grandma alone, travels with the kids on any leg, carry the Government of Canada recommended consent letter (signed by the absent parent, ideally notarized) and the kids' long-form birth certificates.
    • Mexico now uses a digital FMM tourist form on arrival by air. Keep the confirmation.
    • Aruba requires an online ED card before you fly.

    Insurance

    • Buy travel medical insurance for everyone, with trip cancellation and interruption, given the February 2026 Puerto Vallarta disruption.
    • Credit card coverage often has age caps or trip-length limits. Grandma likely needs her own policy with a pre-existing condition stability clause.
    • Many policies exclude regions under "avoid non-essential travel."
    • US medical bills are extreme without insurance, if Florida is in play.

    Car seats and getting around

    • Mexican taxis and package transfers usually don't supply car seats (WestJet Vacations says its transfer company does not). Bring your own (gate-checked free) or book a private transfer that provides them.
    • Mexican car rentals add mandatory local liability insurance, which often doubles the advertised "$10 a day." Plan on about $60 to $90 a day.

    Book

    Little-kid gotchas

    Bottom line

    Low railings, unflagged beaches, unfenced pools and missing cribs catch families off guard. Ask in writing.

    • Balcony railings in Mexico are lower than Canada's (the Government of Canada warns about this). Choose a ground floor or a unit with high railings.
    • Rip currents. Many beaches have no flags or lifeguards, and drownings happen every year. Swim only in sheltered bays.
    • Pool fencing is rare in Mexico. Ask about it before booking.
    • Room occupancy usually counts infants, and cribs are limited, so request them in writing.
    • Medication: bring what you need. The Government of Canada warns about counterfeit medication.
    • Sun and bugs: high-SPF baby sunscreen, swim shirts, and a mosquito net for the pack-and-play.
    • Timeshare pitches at airports and resorts. Under Article 56 of Mexico's Federal Consumer Protection Law and the NOM-029-SE-2021 standard, you can cancel within five business days for any reason; refunds are due within 15 business days, and complaints go to PROFECO.
    • Time zones: Puerto Vallarta and Huatulco are 1 hour behind Toronto, Cabo 2 hours behind, Aruba 1 hour ahead. Easy on a baby's sleep.

    Fine print glossary

    Bottom line

    Listing words hide a lot. Check these eight before comparing prices.

    All-inclusive
    Meals and some drinks and activities bundled. Confirm restaurants, kids' meals, reservations, child charges and whether flights are actually included.
    Room occupancy
    The maximum number of guests allowed, not just the number of beds. A "family room" does not guarantee a separate bedroom.
    Junior suite vs 1-bedroom
    A junior suite is often one large open room. A 1-bedroom suite should have a wall and a door. Check the floor plan.
    Nonstop vs direct
    Nonstop means no landings between Toronto and the destination. A "direct" flight can include a stop. Confirm 0 stops.
    Infant in arms
    Children under 2 can fly without their own seat, but fees may apply. Transport Canada strongly recommends an approved child restraint.
    Beachfront vs beach access
    Beachfront is on the shore but may not be safe to swim. Beach access might mean a long walk, a road crossing or a shuttle.
    Cancellation policy
    The last date for free cancellation and the penalties after. It matters if sargassum, family health or airfare changes.
    Total price
    All required lodging fees plus flights, transfers, meals, bags, child and infant charges, taxes and expected tips.

    Open questions and caveats

    Bottom line

    Read this as a decision guide, not a booking quote. Three questions still decide the shortlist.

    Questions to settle before paying anything

    • Does the budget ceiling include Grandma, or is it for the family of four?
    • Is a walkable swimming beach essential, or is a great pool with occasional beach trips enough?
    • Are all February dates open, or should the trip overlap Family Day and the PA day?

    Caveats

    • Prices are estimates. Airline pages show mostly one-way "from" fares, and live February 2027 package prices weren't published yet. Treat totals as plus or minus 15 to 20%. Exchange rates (USD and MXN) will move rental prices.
    • Live fares are a single snapshot. The week-by-week fares come from one Expedia search on October 12, 2026, in USD converted at 1.38, for the lowest fare type. Use them to compare weeks, not as a quote.
    • Lodging figures are snapshots. The field guide's prices are Booking.com search results from October 12, 2026, and may show the cheapest unit rather than the room type you need.
    • No February 2027 sargassum forecast exists yet. USF issues only month-ahead outlooks, and 2026 was a near-record year with an unusual January landing.
    • Security can change fast. The February 22, 2026 events showed a calm resort area can go to shelter-in-place within hours.
    • Some details (water temperatures for Vallarta and Aruba, Cozumel and Curaçao nonstop schedules, Mazatlán nonstop availability) come from climate data or schedule sites that disagree. Confirm schedules with the airline.
    Field guide's final recommendation

    Start with 7 nights in a 2-bedroom Bucerías or Puerto Vallarta condo, then get a 10-night quote for the same place. Put Huatulco second if its limited nonstop schedule lines up, and Cozumel third if a familiar place appeals. Decide on the exact beds, the bedroom door, flight times and the total family cost.
